<p class="essay-subtitle">
<em>John Muir Trail and Mist Trail</em>
</p>

{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="優勝美地國家公園（Yosemite National Park）位於美國加州內華達山脈西坡，以巍峨花崗岩、原始森林、冰河峽谷和飛瀑流泉聞名於世。群峰環抱之中，默塞德河（Merced River）穿谷而過，冰雪融水奔流不息，千百萬年雕琢出今日雄渾壯麗的山川景觀。每年春夏，雪水自高山傾瀉而下，化作一道道銀練，懸掛於峭壁之間，其中尤以內華達瀑布（Nevada Fall）與維納爾瀑布（Vernal Fall）最負盛名。若欲一日之內盡覽此間精華，則莫過於循約翰·繆爾步道（John Muir Trail）登山，再沿迷霧步道（Mist Trail）下谷。這條不足十公里的環線，兼收雪山、森林、峽谷、激流與飛瀑之勝，被譽為優勝美地最經典的一日健行路線。"
  en="Yosemite National Park, nestled along the western slopes of California's Sierra Nevada, is celebrated throughout the world for its granite cliffs, ancient forests, glacial valleys, and magnificent waterfalls. Through the heart of the valley flows the Merced River, whose snow-fed waters have spent countless millennia carving one of North America's most spectacular landscapes. Each spring and early summer, the melting snow descends from the high country in torrents, transforming sheer granite walls into ribbons of white water. Among Yosemite's many waterfalls, none are more celebrated than Nevada Fall and Vernal Fall. To experience them both in a single day, hikers traditionally ascend by the John Muir Trail and return by the Mist Trail. Though the loop measures less than ten kilometers, it gathers together nearly every landscape that has made Yosemite famous—towering mountains, evergreen forests, rushing rivers, deep granite canyons, and waterfalls of extraordinary power."
%}

{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="這條步道之所以名聞天下，不僅因山川之美，更因一位與優勝美地幾乎畫上等號的人物——約翰·繆爾（John Muir，1838–1914）。這位蘇格蘭裔美國博物學家、作家與自然保護運動先驅，一生幾乎都奉獻給了內華達山脈。他曾獨自在山林間行走數月，觀察冰河遺跡、森林生態與高山植被，並以優美而充滿力量的文字向世人描繪優勝美地的壯麗。他堅信，人不僅需要食物維持生命，更需要大自然滋養靈魂；正是在他的奔走倡議下，優勝美地最終成為美國最重要的國家公園之一，而他也因此被後人尊稱為『美國國家公園之父』。今日無數遊客漫步於約翰·繆爾步道之上，所追尋的不僅是一條山徑，更是一種尊重自然、敬畏天地的人生情懷。"
  en="The trail bears the name of a man whose life has become inseparable from Yosemite itself—John Muir (1838–1914). Born in Scotland and later immigrating to the United States, Muir devoted his life to exploring the Sierra Nevada and became one of the world's most influential advocates for wilderness preservation. He wandered alone through mountains for months at a time, studying glaciers, forests, rivers, and alpine meadows, then transformed those experiences into prose of remarkable beauty and conviction. Muir believed that human beings require more than food and shelter; the soul itself must be nourished by encounters with wild places. Through his writing and tireless advocacy, Yosemite gained lasting protection and eventually became one of America's greatest national parks. Today, every step along the John Muir Trail recalls not only a remarkable naturalist, but also a philosophy that calls people to approach the natural world with humility, gratitude, and reverence."
%}
{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="完整的約翰·繆爾步道全長約三百四十公里，自優勝美地山谷一路向南，穿越內華達山脈高地，終抵美國本土最高峰惠特尼峰（Mount Whitney），為世界最著名的長距離健行步道之一。然而，大多數遊客所走的，只是它的起始一段。自快樂島（Happy Isles）沿默塞德河而上，經克拉克觀景點（Clark Point），抵達內華達瀑布頂端，全程約三點四公里。這一段坡度平緩，道路寬闊，多沿山腰蜿蜒而行。一路既可俯瞰默塞德河峽谷，又可遠望花崗岩峭壁與松林溪谷，視野遼闊，步步皆景，因此也成為遊客最喜愛的登山路線。相較於迷霧步道的濕滑陡峭，約翰·繆爾步道更顯從容寧靜，讓人在徐徐攀升之間，細細體會優勝美地山川的壯闊與深遠。"
  en="The complete John Muir Trail extends approximately 340 kilometers (211 miles), running southward from Yosemite Valley across the high Sierra before ending at Mount Whitney, the highest summit in the contiguous United States. It is widely regarded as one of the world's great long-distance hiking trails. Most visitors, however, experience only its opening section. Beginning at Happy Isles, the trail follows the Merced River, climbs steadily through Clark Point, and reaches the crest of Nevada Fall after about 3.4 kilometers (2.1 miles). The ascent is broad and well graded, tracing the mountainside rather than clinging to the river. Along the way, sweeping vistas unfold across granite domes, pine forests, and the deep Merced canyon below. Unlike the steep and mist-soaked descent of the Mist Trail, the John Muir Trail offers a quieter rhythm, allowing hikers to appreciate Yosemite's grandeur through gradually changing perspectives rather than sudden drama."
%}

{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="若說約翰·繆爾步道展現的是優勝美地遼闊壯麗的一面，那麼迷霧步道則讓人真正走進瀑布之中。自內華達瀑布折返時，多數遊客都會選擇循迷霧步道下山。這條步道沿維納爾瀑布右側花崗岩絕壁開鑿而成，全長約五公里，其中由維納爾瀑布頂至谷底的一段尤為著名。數百級花崗岩石階依山勢層層而下，河水奔騰於咫尺之外，轟鳴之聲震盪山谷。每逢春夏雪融，激流撞擊岩壁，漫天水霧隨風飛散，尚未走近瀑布，衣履早已盡濕。步道因終年瀰漫水霧而得名 Mist Trail（迷霧步道），這也是優勝美地最富盛名的一段健行路線。晴朗的午後，陽光穿過飛濺的水珠，彩虹凌空而起，橫跨瀑前，如七彩長橋懸於天地之間。人在其中，既聞雷霆萬鈞，又見煙霞流彩，不禁令人讚嘆造化之神奇。"
  en="If the John Muir Trail reveals Yosemite through broad panoramas, the Mist Trail invites visitors into the very heart of the waterfalls themselves. Most hikers choose it for the descent from Nevada Fall. Carved directly into the granite cliffs beside Vernal Fall, the trail stretches about 5.1 kilometers (3.2 miles), with its most celebrated section descending from the top of Vernal Fall to the valley floor. Hundreds of massive stone steps follow the contours of the cliff while the Merced River rushes only a few meters away. During spring and early summer, when snowmelt reaches its peak, exploding spray rises high above the gorge. Long before reaching the waterfall, hikers find themselves wrapped in cool mist, their clothes dampened by the drifting water. It is this ever-present veil of spray that gave the trail its name. On bright afternoons, sunlight refracts through countless droplets, casting brilliant rainbows across the gorge. Surrounded by thunderous water, drifting mist, and vivid color, one experiences not merely a hike, but a profound encounter with the living power of the mountains."
%}
{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="內華達瀑布（Nevada Fall）高約一百八十一公尺，為優勝美地最高、也是最具代表性的瀑布之一。Nevada 一詞源自西班牙語，意為「覆雪」或「積雪」，正呼應了默塞德河源頭終年積雪的高山。河水自上游緩緩流過大片花崗岩平台，平靜得幾乎令人忘記它即將墜落。走過瀑頂木橋，只見河床忽然收束，原本寬闊的水面瞬間匯聚成一道急流，在短短數十公尺內驟然加速，隨即凌空飛瀉而下。巨大的水柱撞擊谷底岩石，激起漫天白霧，轟鳴之聲迴盪山谷，即使相隔數百公尺，仍可感受到腳下岩石微微震動。站在瀑頂俯視，只見激流如萬馬奔騰，頃刻間沒入深谷；回首遠望，又見整座瀑布懸掛於花崗岩峭壁之間，雄渾壯麗，令人屏息。"
  en="Nevada Fall plunges approximately 181 meters (594 feet), making it one of Yosemite's tallest and most commanding waterfalls. Its name comes from the Spanish word *nevada*, meaning 'snow-covered,' an apt tribute to the snowfields that nourish the Merced River throughout much of the year. Above the brink, the river glides quietly across broad granite slabs with almost deceptive calm, as though unaware of the immense drop awaiting it. Crossing the footbridge at the crest, visitors witness a remarkable transformation. The broad river suddenly narrows, gathering its entire force into a powerful torrent that accelerates within only a few dozen meters before hurling itself into open space. The impact below sends towering clouds of spray into the air, while the roar reverberates throughout the canyon. Even from a considerable distance, the vibration seems to travel through the granite beneath one's feet. Standing beside the brink, one looks down upon water disappearing into the depths below; looking back, the entire waterfall hangs like a white banner against Yosemite's immense granite walls."
%}
![內華達瀑布](/assets/images/xinglv/nevada.jpg)
{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="若說內華達瀑布令人敬畏，那麼維納爾瀑布（Vernal Fall）則更容易令人親近。它高約九十七公尺，雖然只有內華達瀑布約一半的高度，卻因迷霧步道幾乎貼著瀑布而行，使人得以近距離感受激流奔騰的力量。Vernal 意為「春天的」，正如其名，每逢春季雪水豐沛之時，整座瀑布如白龍出谷，飛流直下。水流猛烈撞擊岩壁，霧珠瀰漫空中，陽光照射之下，彩虹幾乎日日可見。遊人一步一步登上花崗岩石階，耳畔唯有瀑布震耳欲聾的轟鳴，眼前唯有漫天飛霧與橫空彩虹。有人駐足凝望，有人靜坐岩石之上，也有人久久不願離去。此時此刻，人彷彿不再只是觀賞瀑布，而是成為山川景色的一部分。"
  en="If Nevada Fall inspires awe through its overwhelming scale, Vernal Fall leaves an equally lasting impression through intimacy. Rising approximately 97 meters (317 feet), it is only about half as tall as its larger neighbor, yet many visitors remember it more vividly because the Mist Trail passes almost beside the waterfall itself. The word *vernal* means 'spring-like,' and nowhere is that name more fitting than during the season of melting snow. Then the Merced River pours over the cliff with astonishing force, filling the surrounding air with countless droplets that drift across the trail. Under clear skies, rainbows often appear as naturally as the mist itself. Step by step, hikers climb the granite staircase with the thunder of falling water echoing beside them. Conversation fades beneath the roar, replaced by the sound of rushing water and the cool breath of the spray. Some pause only briefly, while others remain for long moments, quietly watching the endless movement of river, light, and stone. Here one does not merely observe nature; one becomes, if only for a little while, part of its unfolding rhythm."
%}

![維納爾瀑布](/assets/images/xinglv/vernal.jpg)
     {% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="內華達瀑布與維納爾瀑布同屬默塞德河，卻各具風姿。內華達瀑布之美，在於雄渾磅礴；河水由平緩而急促，積蓄千鈞之勢，凌空飛瀉一百八十餘公尺，令人感受到大自然不可抗拒的力量。維納爾瀑布則勝於靈動親近。人行瀑側，水霧拂面，彩虹橫空，飛瀑彷彿觸手可及。前者令人心生敬畏，後者令人流連忘返；一剛一柔，一遠一近，共同構成了優勝美地最動人的山水畫卷。"
  en="Although both waterfalls belong to the Merced River, each possesses a distinct personality. Nevada Fall impresses through sheer power. Its quiet current gathers irresistible force before plunging more than 180 meters into the canyon below, reminding visitors of nature's overwhelming strength. Vernal Fall, by contrast, is remembered for its intimacy. The trail passes so close to the cascading water that cool mist brushes one's face while rainbows seem almost within reach. One inspires reverence, the other invites companionship. Together they reveal two expressions of the same river—one majestic and commanding, the other graceful and welcoming—forming one of Yosemite's most unforgettable landscapes."
%}

{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="一年四季，步道景色各不相同。暮春與初夏，冰雪初融，河水最盛，瀑布如萬馬奔騰，是一年中最壯觀的時節；盛夏天高氣爽，松林蒼翠，最宜健行遠足；秋日水勢漸緩，花崗岩絕壁完全顯露，金黃橡樹點綴其間，更添幾分寧靜；冬季白雪覆山，部分步道因積雪封閉，瀑布邊緣偶結冰凌，天地寂然，別具一番清冷肅穆之美。山川雖未改變，卻因季節輪轉而展現不同氣象，也使每一次重返優勝美地，都成為一次全新的相遇。"
  en="The character of the trail changes with every season. In late spring and early summer, melting snow transforms the Merced River into a torrent, and the waterfalls reach their greatest power. Summer brings clear skies, fragrant pine forests, and ideal conditions for hiking. By autumn, the river grows quieter, revealing polished granite cliffs framed by golden oaks and maples. Winter covers the high country with snow, closing portions of the trail while delicate ice forms along the edges of the falls, lending the valley a solemn stillness. Though the mountains themselves remain unchanged, every season reveals another face of Yosemite, ensuring that no two visits are ever truly the same."
%}

{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="今日，人們來到優勝美地，多半是為了欣賞壯麗風景，拍下一張張照片，留下旅途的紀念。然而，真正令人難忘的，往往並非照片裡的山峰與瀑布，而是身處其間時那份難以言喻的感受。當耳邊只剩流水與風聲，當目光越過峽谷遙望層巒疊嶂，人便會忽然明白，自然從不急於向人展示自己的偉大，它只是靜靜存在，而我們有幸在生命中的某一天，與它相遇。也許，這正是約翰·繆爾終其一生所希望告訴世人的道理：走進山林，不只是為了欣賞風景，更是為了重新認識自己。"
  en="Most people come to Yosemite hoping to admire its celebrated scenery and return home with beautiful photographs. Yet the memories that endure are seldom the images preserved by a camera. What remains instead is the quiet feeling of having stood within a landscape far greater than oneself. When conversation gives way to the sound of rushing water and mountain wind, and when one's eyes follow the granite walls toward distant summits, an unexpected realization often emerges: nature never strives to impress us. It simply exists, patiently and magnificently, waiting for those willing to slow their steps. Perhaps this is the lesson John Muir hoped others would discover. We enter the mountains not merely to see beautiful places, but to recover a deeper understanding of ourselves."
%}

{% include xinghe/paragraph.html
  zh="離開山谷時，我回首望向默塞德河。河水依舊向前奔流，瀑布依舊日夜轟鳴，花崗岩依舊靜默地矗立在天地之間。人來人往，不過是山川漫長歲月中的一瞬；而山河也正因無言，才更顯永恆。或許多年以後，關於旅程中的許多細節早已淡忘，但當再次想起優勝美地時，心中浮現的，仍將是那奔騰的河流、飛瀑的白霧，以及陽光下那一道橫跨山谷的彩虹。"
  en="As I left the valley, I turned once more toward the Merced River. The water continued its timeless journey, the waterfalls roared without pause, and the granite cliffs stood in quiet permanence beneath the open sky. Countless travelers come and go, each passing through for only a fleeting moment, while the mountains remain almost beyond the measure of time. Perhaps that is why they move us so deeply. Years from now, many details of the journey will inevitably fade, yet Yosemite will return to memory not as a collection of landmarks, but as the sound of living water, the cool breath of mist, and a rainbow suspended above the valley—a reminder that some places do not simply delight the eye; they quietly enlarge the human spirit."
